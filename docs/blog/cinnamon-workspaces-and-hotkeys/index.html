Workspaces

A layout per workspace.

Workspaces are the oldest way to keep a desktop tidy: four screens, each with its own windows. What Cinnamon does not do is let those screens look different from each other. Here are the defaults — and how to put a different layout on every workspace.

greenTile adds what Cinnamon leaves out: a layout per workspace that comes back on its own, with the panel, the switcher and the hot corners untouched.

The defaults A layout per workspace

What Cinnamon gives you.

Defaults in Cinnamon 6.x, read from Cinnamon's own settings schemas.

  • Four workspacesCreated up front, not on demand. The settings schema allows up to 36, and Cinnamon's own workspace settings are in System Settings → Workspaces.
  • SwitchCtrlAlt+←/→ moves to the workspace next door.
  • Take the window alongCtrlAltShift+←/→ sends the focused window over and follows it.
  • Jump directlyThe numbered shortcuts are unbound by default; assign Super+1…4 in the keyboard settings and you never cycle again.
  • See where you areThe workspace switcher applet in the panel, and the workspace name that can be flashed on screen while switching.
  • Expo and ScaleAn overview of all workspaces, or of the windows on the current one. Both reachable from the hot corners, which ship switched off.

Everything in that list lives in two places in System Settings: the workspace settings decide how many workspaces exist and whether their names flash on screen while switching, while the keyboard page holds the shortcuts and the Hot Corners panel holds the four corners. None of it needs an extension — and none of it says anything about what the windows inside a workspace should look like.

What workspaces do not do.

A workspace is a place, not a plan. Everything about how the windows are arranged inside it is the result of what you did by hand, and it belongs to the session rather than to the workspace:

  • No arrangement of its own. Move a window in workspace 2, and workspaces 1 and 3 do not know or care.
  • Nothing arrives ready. Switch to an empty workspace and the screen is empty. The first two windows still land wherever the applications put them.
  • The half-tiling is manual, too. Super+arrows pushes one window at a time — see what Cinnamon does on its own.
  • Nothing carries over. Every workspace, every day, one window at a time.

The reason is structural, and worth one sentence: a window's position is a property of the window, and a workspace is little more than the list of which windows are on it. Neither of the two stores an arrangement. That is why the desktop behaves like a desk rather than a template — the piles are wherever you last put them, and nothing wrote down where they belong.

If that is exactly how you like it, you are finished here. If your three workspaces are "writing", "browsing" and "chat", each with a shape you keep rebuilding, the rest of this page is for you.

A layout per workspace.

greenTile keeps its settings per monitor and per workspace: preset, auto state, remembered borders. Workspace 1 can be one wide column while workspace 2 is a main window with a list beside it and workspace 3 stays free for floating windows.

  1. Install greenTile from the release zip, restart Cinnamon, enable it in the Extensions manager.
  2. Open the preset panel on the workspace you are setting up: SuperCtrlP. Pick one of the sixteen starters or paint your own on the 6 × 4 grid.
  3. Assign the preset to the monitor and workspace. Switch to the next workspace and repeat — the panel always edits the workspace you are on.
  4. Turn auto mode on for the workspaces that should look after themselves: SuperCtrlA on, SuperCtrlD off. Leave a workspace without auto mode and it stays a free surface for windows you place by hand.
  5. Work normally. Switch between workspaces as you always did; each one brings its own arrangement with it.

Three workspaces, then, as three concrete assignments. Workspace 1 gets one full-width column and auto mode on: whatever you open there is fitted into it, and the workspace is a writing desk with a single sheet on it. Workspace 2 gets the main-plus-list shape, the browser wide on the left and the mail client narrow on the right, so a message can be read without leaving the page it is about. Workspace 3 stays without auto mode and without a preset — a free surface for the windows the afternoon brings. Switching between them is the same CtrlAlt+←/→ as before; what changes is that each switch lands on a desk that is already arranged.

That is the part no window manager shortcut can do for you: the workspace remembers the shape of your work, not just the windows you left there. A monitor that gets replugged or a Cinnamon that gets restarted finds its assignment again and puts the windows back in the order you left them.

What you gain over a place.

The same four workspaces, each with a shape of its own.

A plan, not a pile

The workspace is arranged already

A preset is assigned to the workspace and comes back with it. Switching to workspace 2 lands on the arrangement you left there, not on four windows to sort out again.

On its own

It looks after itself

With auto mode on, a window that opens joins the arrangement and a window that closes leaves its space to the neighbours. Off, the workspace stays a free surface — useful for the one you use for a single big window.

Per monitor

Each screen, each workspace

The settings are kept per monitor and per workspace, so the laptop screen and the external one can carry different arrangements of the same workspace, and a replugged monitor finds its layout again.

Still Cinnamon

Every key you know still works

The panel, the workspace switcher, the hot corners and CtrlAlt+arrows are untouched. greenTile adds a shape to a workspace; it does not replace the way you move between them.

Extensions that help with workspaces.

Not tiling, but the same corner of the desktop. All three are in the Spices catalogue.

auto-move-windows

Apps find their workspace

Moves applications to specific workspaces when they create windows, so your browser opens on workspace 2 by itself.

declared for Cinnamon 4.0 – 5.6
RearrangeWorkspaces

Reorder them

Rearranges the workspaces themselves and takes their windows along — handy when the busy one ended up fourth.

declared for Cinnamon 5.4
workspace-scroller

Scroll to switch

Switches workspaces when you scroll with the mouse at the screen edge.

declared for Cinnamon 2.4 – 4.4

The compatibility column is worth a second look: these three were written before Cinnamon 6 and still declare older versions. They generally keep working, but they are not maintained for current releases. More on the state of the catalogue in the tiling extension overview.
